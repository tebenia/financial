# Family Financial Life Plan

**Status:** Preliminary planning brief  
**Prepared:** 2026-09-23  
**Review cadence:** At least annually and after a major family, employment, health, or regulatory change

## Purpose

The household does not currently have a single purchase target. Its broader objective is:

> Protect a one-income family, preserve purchasing power, prepare for Hajj and the children's education, and gradually build enough financial freedom that travel, health problems, and old age do not create persistent financial worry.

Investment is therefore a supporting tool, not the objective itself. The aim is not to become rich quickly or to maximize return at any cost. The aim is to preserve and gradually improve the family's real purchasing power while keeping essential goals safe.

## Current household context

The following facts are known at this preliminary stage:

- The household primarily depends on one employment income.
- The spouse is currently a homemaker.
- There are two children, both currently in elementary school.
- The family already owns a home.
- The household currently has no debt.
- A car is not an immediate need.
- Current health protection includes BPJS.
- Important future possibilities include Hajj, Umrah, family travel, children's secondary and university education, and retirement.
- A scholarship to a Japanese university is an aspiration for the children, but the financial plan must also support realistic partial-scholarship and no-scholarship alternatives.
- The preferred financial posture is safe and conservative.
- There is also a strong interest in learning how to analyze companies, identify good and bad investments, and make limited speculative decisions from evidence.

Exact income, balances, account details, and other sensitive household figures are intentionally excluded from this public planning document. They should be stored only under the Git-ignored `private/` directory.

## Preliminary risk assessment

The household's provisional profile is:

> **Conservative long-term investor with strong intellectual curiosity about stocks.**

Two aspects of risk must remain separate:

1. **Risk preference:** The investor prefers safety but finds markets and company selection intellectually interesting.
2. **Risk capacity:** A one-income household with two dependent children has limited capacity to expose essential money to major losses.

Risk capacity governs the family portfolio. Curiosity can be served through a small, separately bounded learning portfolio.

## Financial priority structure

Money should be assigned to six distinct buckets:

1. Monthly operating cash
2. Family emergency reserve
3. Health and income protection
4. Hajj, education, and travel funds
5. Long-term retirement and inflation-protection portfolio
6. Stock-learning and speculation account

Each bucket has a different purpose and time horizon, so the same investment product should not automatically be used for all of them.

## 1. Monthly operating cash

This is money needed for routine household expenses over approximately the next one or two months. Its purpose is reliability and liquidity, not maximum return.

Not every rupiah must beat inflation every year. Cash also functions as insurance: it prevents forced investment sales during emergencies or market declines.

## 2. Family emergency reserve

Because the household depends primarily on one income and supports two children, the preliminary emergency-fund target is:

```text
Emergency-fund target = essential monthly expenses x 9 to 12 months
```

This target must be calculated after essential monthly expenditure is documented. The reserve may eventually be layered between:

- Immediately accessible bank cash for the first one or two months
- Deposits or a suitable liquid money-market instrument for additional months
- Another conservative and accessible instrument for the remainder

The emergency reserve must not be placed in individual stocks, cryptoassets, illiquid products, or instruments whose value may be materially depressed when the money is urgently needed.

## 3. Health and income protection

BPJS is the household's protection foundation. Before purchasing additional products, the family should review:

- Whether every family member's BPJS membership is active
- Accessibility and suitability of nearby BPJS facilities
- Any supplementary health, life, accident, disability, or pension benefits provided through employment
- The financial consequences if the income earner cannot work temporarily or permanently
- The financial consequences if the income earner dies before the children become independent
- Whether faster access, private hospitalization, or private-room coverage is desired

Possible private insurance should be compared as protection first. Relevant terms include annual limits, procedure sublimits, deductibles, co-payments, hospital networks, cashless service, waiting periods, pre-existing-condition exclusions, renewability, age-related premium increases, and coordination with BPJS.

Insurance combined with investment should not be accepted or rejected merely by product category. Its protection, fees, surrender value, exclusions, and projections must be compared transparently with purchasing protection and investing separately.

## 4. Goal-specific funds

### Hajj

Hajj is a genuine financial goal even though the departure year is not yet specified.

As a 2026 reference point, the initial regular-Hajj deposit is Rp25 million per person. BPKH reported an average 2026 Bipih paid directly by a pilgrim of approximately Rp54.19 million, with the actual amount differing by embarkation. These are dated planning references, not forecasts of the family's eventual cost.

The next planning question is whether both adults have already paid the initial deposit and received queue numbers. If neither has registered, the current initial-deposit reference for two adults is:

```text
Rp25 million x 2 adults = Rp50 million
```

This is a registration reference, not the complete future Hajj budget. A later budget must also consider settlement, equipment, medical preparation, transport, and incidental costs. Future rules and costs must be refreshed from official sources.

### Children's education

Scholarships should be pursued but treated as upside rather than the household's only plan. Education planning will use at least three scenarios:

| Scenario | Household responsibility |
|---|---|
| Full scholarship | Preparation, applications, travel, insurance, equipment, and emergency support |
| Partial scholarship | Some tuition and a meaningful share of living expenses |
| No scholarship | A financially realistic Indonesian university or another affordable route |

The education strategy should:

1. Fund academic, language, and application preparation.
2. Preserve a credible Indonesian university fallback.
3. Build a partial overseas-study reserve when affordable.
4. Recalculate the target annually as each child's interests and abilities become clearer.
5. Reduce investment risk as university enrollment approaches.

Future costs should be tested under multiple education-cost inflation assumptions rather than relying on a single forecast:

```text
Future education cost = current estimated cost x (1 + assumed annual cost growth)^years
```

### Travel and Umrah

Travel is part of the desired life and should be planned rather than treated as an accidental expense. Each trip should use a separate sinking-fund calculation:

```text
Monthly contribution = estimated future trip cost / months until departure
```

Estimates should include inflation, exchange-rate uncertainty, insurance, and ticket-price variability. Money required within approximately one to three years should normally remain in cash-like or short-duration conservative instruments rather than individual stocks.

## 5. Retirement and inflation protection

The long-term objective is to preserve and gradually increase purchasing power after fees, taxes, inflation, and losses. It is not necessary or realistic for every asset to beat inflation every year.

A future diversified retirement portfolio may combine:

- Cash and deposits for liquidity
- Government securities or retail SBN
- Carefully selected bond or money-market funds
- Diversified equity exposure for long-term growth
- A strictly limited allocation to individually researched companies

The retirement calculation must account for actual employment and pension benefits. Employment category, pension rights, Taspen or equivalent coverage, retirement age, and expected benefits remain to be documented privately before a target allocation is selected.

## 6. Stock-learning and speculation account

Company analysis is a valid educational objective, but it must not endanger family goals. A separate learning account should follow these provisional boundaries:

- Use no borrowed money or margin.
- Never use emergency, health, Hajj, education, or near-term travel money.
- Keep the account small enough that a total loss would not change any family plan.
- As an initial ceiling, consider no more than 5% of genuinely investable financial assets; the appropriate amount may be lower.
- Base the percentage on assets remaining after safety reserves and near-term obligations, not on the total bank balance.
- Require a written thesis before every purchase.
- Record the expected value, valuation, risks, disconfirming evidence, and exit conditions.
- Include fees, taxes, dividends, and idle cash when measuring performance.
- Compare results with a suitable benchmark.
- Review decision quality, not only whether a trade happened to make money.

The learning program should distinguish a good company from a good investment:

```text
Investment attractiveness
= business quality
+ financial strength
+ durable prospects
+ governance quality
+ a reasonable purchase price
```

A strong company purchased at an excessive valuation can be a poor investment. A statistically cheap company with deteriorating fundamentals can be a value trap.

## Provisional decision rules

Until the household numbers are complete:

1. Do not select a final asset allocation.
2. Do not invest money assigned to essential or near-term goals in individual stocks.
3. Do not treat recent returns as evidence that a product is safe.
4. Do not buy a product whose risks, liquidity, fees, taxation, and exit conditions are not understood.
5. Verify the legal status of providers and products through the relevant official authority.
6. Keep credentials, account numbers, statements, and exact household balances out of Git and notebooks intended for publication.
7. Treat all return projections as uncertain scenarios, not promises.

## Current conclusion

The first objective is not to maximize returns or identify the next winning stock. It is to determine essential monthly expenditure and construct a complete family safety floor. Investments should then be matched to the date and purpose for which each amount will be needed.

The intended structure is:

```text
FAMILY CORE
|- Operating cash
|- Emergency reserve
|- Health and income protection
|- Hajj reserve
|- Education reserves
|- Travel sinking funds
`- Diversified retirement portfolio

LEARNING SATELLITE
|- Company research
|- Valuation exercises
|- Small stock positions
`- Written performance review
```

This allows the household to remain financially conservative while developing serious investment knowledge.
